01 Entries · 2005–2025

Twenty years of arrivals. Three times the gate closed.

In 2005 Australia counted 346,000 international enrolments. In 2025 it counted 1.06 million. The line between them is not a ramp. It stalled after 2009, fell off a cliff when the borders shut in March 2020, and in 2025 fell again, this time with the borders wide open. That fall was policy, not a pandemic: visa fees nearly tripled and new students were capped.

02 Country of citizenship

The biggest senders aren't the keenest.

China and India have topped the list every year since 2005. Measure each against its own population and both drop into the thirties. Bhutan has enrolments in Australia equal to one in every 51 of its people. If Australia did the same, more than half a million Australians would be studying in a single foreign country.

03 Course of study

Your passport predicts your degree.

Four in ten international enrolments are in business or IT. Domestic students spread themselves far more evenly. And the mix depends on where you're from: a third of Brazilian and Colombian students are here to learn English, while Bangladeshi students choose IT nearly two times in five.

04 Address in Australia

Different flags, different cities.

Almost half of Chinese students live in New South Wales. The largest share of Indian students is in Victoria. Nepali students, more than any other group, choose Sydney. Where a student comes from shapes which city they land in, and which campus they share.

05 Conditions

Bigger than gold. Easier to lose.

Students arrive in two waves, in February and July. In 2020 the February wave landed before the borders shut, and the July wave halved. In 2025 both waves were smaller than the year before. For an industry that earns more than gold, that is not a rounding error.
